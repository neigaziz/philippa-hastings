# Doctoral studentship assessment

Use for BBSRC DTP, rotation, iCASE and project-based doctoral applications. Published competition criteria override this default model.

## Default weighted framework

| Criterion | Weight | Evidence to examine |
|---|---:|---|
| Academic preparation | 30% | Degree results and trajectory; relevant modules; technical and transferable skills; dissertation, placements, internships and outputs; actual research contribution |
| Research motivation and programme fit | 25% | Specific scientific interests; BBSRC remit; understanding of training structure and values; why this programme/project rather than any PhD; relevant funding context |
| Personal and professional attributes | 20% | Evidenced resilience, reflection, collaboration and inclusion; cohort engagement, public engagement or widening participation where relevant; realistic career awareness |
| Project feasibility and fit | 15% | Research question and methods; independent thought beyond the advert; relevant host resources; for rotations, breadth and informed project choice |
| Communication and writing quality | 10% | Clarity, structure, scientific accuracy; formal references if supplied; interview reasoning and engagement only when assessed |

Score each available criterion 0–5: 0 = no demonstrated evidence in the submitted material; 1 = weak; 2 = partial; 3 = credible; 4 = strong; 5 = exceptional for this career stage. Explain every rating using evidence. Missing references or an unheld interview are unavailable, not poor performances. If the coverage is incomplete, report provisional scores and identify the affected criteria; do not quietly renormalise weights.

Weighted total out of 100 = sum(weight × rating / 5). Out of 10 = total / 10. These numbers are illustrative; no actual shortlisting threshold or probability follows from them.

## 60-second application scan

- Degree meets the stated requirement, including accepted equivalence or contextual routes; 2:1 is a common example, not a universal rule.
- Evidence of research experience: laboratory, field, computational, theoretical or other relevant work.
- Programme named and specific project/theme identified where the form requests it.
- Scientific interests connect to the relevant council remit.
- Skills have context, proficiency and evidence of contribution.
- Referees meet the scheme requirements and can provide relevant evidence.

## PIER personal statement model

**P — Previous Research:** What question did you investigate? What did you do, with which methods, at what level of independence? What result, difficulty or limitation shaped your thinking?

**I — Intellectual Interest:** What scientific question now motivates you? How does it connect to the programme's actual themes?

**E — Engagement with Programme:** Why this programme/project? Which verified training, rotation, cohort, placement or supervisory features address your needs?

**R — Readiness and Goals:** What preparation and transferable skills support doctoral work? What training remains necessary, and what realistic research-focused career direction motivates the next step?

Adapt PIER to the actual application questions; do not force four paragraphs into an incompatible form.

## Red flags and fair interpretation

Investigate absent research evidence, long irrelevant employment descriptions, skills lists without context, generic programme references, referees unable to discuss relevant aptitude and unchecked international eligibility. Lack of laboratory experience is not lack of research experience. Publications, research placements and extensive professional networks are not universal entry requirements. Relate opportunity and circumstances to demonstrated potential.

## Training-model fit

Rotation: evaluate breadth, curiosity and capacity to choose a project after exploration. DTP/cohort: evaluate fit with the actual training model and collaboration. iCASE: evaluate scientific contribution, understanding of the partner, collaborative working and placement objectives. Project studentship: evaluate preparation for the defined question and scope to develop intellectual ownership. Recommend a route using the candidate's evidence and goals, not presumed prestige.
